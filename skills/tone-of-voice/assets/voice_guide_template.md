## 6. Marketing voice vs. Service voice

### 6.1 Marketing voice has more latitude

Marketing copy is where the archetype shows up fully. The Lover can be sensual, the Outlaw can be transgressive, the Magician can promise transformation. We have room to lean in.

### 6.2 Service voice dials back

When a customer is in a service moment — they need help, something went wrong, they have a question — personality is friction. Service voice keeps the archetype *recognisable* but removes anything that would slow them down or feel performative.

**Specifically:**
- [3–4 dial-back rules for this brand, e.g., "No jokes in service contexts. The Jester sits down."]
- [E.g., "Keep sentence length short — comprehension first."]
- [E.g., "Use plain words, even where marketing would reach for richer language."]

## 8. Quick checklist

Before you hit send, scan this:

- [ ] Does the first sentence make the point, or am I building up to it?
- [ ] Have I used "we" instead of [brand name]?
- [ ] Is there an empty adjective ("amazing", "incredible", "premium") I can cut?
- [ ] Does the rhythm sound like a person, not a press release?
- [ ] Would the [Primary] archetype actually say this?
- [ ] If it's a service comm, have I dialled back the archetype?
- [ ] Is there a specific noun where I've used a vague one?
