# Jobs-to-be-Done — quick reference

Use this in step 2 of the workflow. JTBD (Clayton Christensen) reframes "who is the customer" as "what job is the customer hiring this product to do."

## The three jobs — always name all three

### Functional job
The practical, task-level outcome. What's the literal thing that needs to happen?
- Starts with a verb: "Get to work on time", "Find a safe place to live", "Feel presentable for the meeting."
- Usually the easiest to articulate and the least differentiating.

### Emotional job
How they want to feel, or avoid feeling, in the process.
- "Feel in control", "Feel prepared", "Avoid feeling foolish", "Feel like a good parent."
- Often more powerful than the functional job. Almost never shows up in a functional brief.

### Social job
How they want to be perceived by others — or how they want to perceive themselves in relation to others.
- "Be seen as a provider", "Not burden my kids", "Signal that I'm serious about my career", "Belong to the group of people who shop here."
- Often the *hidden* driver in category decisions. Interrogate this especially hard.

## How to find the jobs

- **Look for struggling moments.** JTBD lives in moments where the current solution is failing. What triggers consideration?
- **Ask "and then what?"** until you hit emotion or identity. "I need a retirement home" → "...so I don't fall" → "...so I stay independent" → "...so I don't have to ask my kids for help" (that's the social job).
- **Notice the hire/fire language.** What were they using before? Why did they fire it? What got them to hire this?
- **Listen for metaphors.** "It's like a noose around my neck", "It felt like a weight off", "I feel seen" — metaphors betray emotional jobs.

## Common traps

- **Confusing the job with the product.** The job isn't "buy a mattress." The job is "wake up feeling like I can handle the day."
- **Writing only the functional job.** Every brief has it. You need emotional and social to unlock creative.
- **Projecting your own jobs onto the audience.** If the insight feels obvious to you, it might be yours, not theirs.
- **Picking a job that's too broad.** "Be happy" isn't a job. "Feel like I made the right choice for mum" is.

## Worked example — prospective retirement home resident

- **Functional:** Secure a living arrangement that provides daily care and safety as physical capability declines.
- **Emotional:** Retain a sense of autonomy and dignity; avoid feeling like a patient or an imposition.
- **Social:** Continue to be perceived (by self, family, peers) as a capable adult and parent — *the one who provides, not the one who is provided for.*

Notice how the social job flips the whole brief. "Retirement living" in most category ads is sold as a *relief* to families — unwittingly positioning the resident as a burden lifted. The social job says: sell it to the resident as an act of *their* continued provision for the family. Very different creative.

## Deliverable format

Write each job as a short sentence, with who's feeling it and the emotional payoff. Then write one line on what would make the job feel "done well" — because success criteria drive the messaging frame.
